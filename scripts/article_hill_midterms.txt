The dust has not yet settled from the seismic midterm elections, but the contours of the Democratic triumph are becoming clearer.

Several key demographic groups have moved toward Democrats since President Trump defeated then-Vice President Kamala Harris two years ago. In aggregate, these shifts powered a massive repudiation of Trump and the GOP.

As of Wednesday evening, Democrats had won at least 54 Senate seats, while The Hill's data and polling partner Decision Desk HQ is projecting the party will end up with 261 House seats. This would put the Democrats above their high-water mark of the Obama years, 257.

A more granular look at the election is possible because of two major studies. One is the voter trends survey conducted by The New York Times, and the other is the voter poll commissioned by seven other major news organizations and carried out by SSRS.

Hispanic voters shifted sharply away from the GOP. In 2024, the Times voter survey showed Trump winning 44 percent of Latino voters against Harris. This year, the New York Times survey showed the GOP winning just 30 percent of Hispanic voters, while the SSRS voter poll put the number at 34 percent. A striking 50 percent of all voters said they strongly disapprove of ICE's performance and 94 percent of that cohort backed the Democrats.

Men moved against the GOP even more than women. In 2024, women backed Harris by 10 points over Trump while men favored him by 15 points. The shift was somewhat more pronounced among men than women. Men overall broke for the Democrats, by 3 points in the Times survey and 2 points in SSRS. The Times data indicated an 18-point swing toward Democrats among men, whereas the equivalent shift among women was just 13 points.

Young people shift strongly back to Democrats. Two years ago, Trump won 43 or 44 percent of voters under the age of 30. This year, the SSRS poll showed the GOP won just 21 percent of voters under the age of 25, and 28 percent of those aged 25-29. The New York Times survey pegged Republican support among voters under 30 at 28 percent.

GOP's previous advances with Black voters also stall. The SSRS voter poll showed Black voters going for Democrats by an 81-point margin, 90 points to 9 points. The New York Times survey showed a similar pattern, with Democrats prevailing 91-8.

GOP shows more resilience among non-college whites. The SSRS survey suggested white men without a college education went for the GOP on Tuesday by almost 30 points, 63-35. Their female counterparts favored Republicans by 16 points, 57-41. The Times survey noted this group still swung toward Democrats by 14 points since Trump's 2024 win.
